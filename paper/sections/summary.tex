\begin{leptonsummary}
{\color{leptonnavy}\large\bfseries EXECUTIVE SUMMARY\par}
\vspace{1mm}
A few days' warning before a storm lets health offices send doxycycline to the barangays most
likely to flood; a week's warning lets hospitals prepare for the cases that follow. Project
LEPTON gives Quezon City (QC) both from one flood computation: NASA IMERG rain over the city's
catchments, set against Project NOAH flood maps and WorldPop population fitted to the census,
estimates how many residents of each barangay a storm exposes. Layer~1, a negative binomial
regression on season, recent cases, rain and a flood exposure index, forecasts next week's cases
with a 90\% range and the probability of crossing the epidemic threshold. In a 2018--2026 weekly
backtest on QC's case counts, hiding the latest two weeks as if unreported, it missed by 6.70
cases a week over 349 forecasts outside the COVID-19 years, against 15.59 for our reproduction
of the published Prophet--XGBoost hybrid: 8.88 fewer (95\% block-bootstrap interval 1.88 to
19.93). It also beat a seasonal average, and from 2022 its 90\% ranges held 96\% of counts. The
flood index did not measurably improve it. Layer~2 turns exposed residents into doxycycline,
children's courses and amoxicillin per barangay and health center, under DOH
Memorandum~\mbox{2023-0309}. On four storms, day-ahead ECMWF forecasts put 25\% of residents in
flooded zones for Typhoon Carina, against 0.05\% on satellite rain, which under-read it; for the
July 2025 floods they fell short. Without barangay case counts, these runs are demonstrations,
not validations. All dose figures are for stock planning, not prescriptions.
